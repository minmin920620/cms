INTRODUCTION
Project Context
Crime recording and monitoring is essential in ensuring peace and security within a community. As such, police departments are reliant on accurate data and well-maintained records of crimes committed to track down and prosecute offenders. It is therefore important that police departments have proper and updated records of all crimes. With the rise of modern technology, most organizations and government agencies are embracing technology to manage and store data digitally and make it easily accessible. Some organizations are using crime mapping technology also known as GIS to record and monitor crime patterns and locations on maps.
The Philippine National Police in the Koronadal is a government agency tasked with providing security to the people of Koronadal City in South Cotabato. Their duty includes regulating and enforcing laws in the city and taking action after a crime or a report of a crime has been confirmed. The police station is answerable to the Local Government Unit of the city and provides services such as recording, documenting and investigating criminal activity and cases and providing assistanceto individuals and businesses inregardsto crime prevention and response within the region.
At present, PNP Koronadal Station utilizes a computerized but manual method in recording and monitoring of crime incident. Encoding of data in computer database is done manually while some data are retrieved from written or manual reports. Information such as type of offense committed, date and time of incident, and location of incident among others are being recorded and stored for monitoring, reference and report generation purposes. In addition, the location of the incident is manually recorded or provided in written or narrative form.
However, the current process has a lot of drawbacks in terms of recording and monitoring of crime. The process of encoding crime incident details is tedious and can cause missing information, incorrect recording, and errors. The retrieval of past crime incident records might be difficult when the file is not properly indexed. Determining the hotspots is difficult due to the absence of a map feature where the investigator is able to indicate the approximate location or exact spot of the crime occurrence. A report generation based on type of crime, barangay, status, and period of occurrence requires additional time and effort.
These problems negatively affect the efficiency of the PNP Koronadal Station in addressing crime-related issues. Police personnel waste a lot of their time by organizing and preparing reports instead of doing actual supervisory duties. Moreover, the process of accessing information is too slow, making it difficult for the officers to make decisions regarding crime patterns. In addition, for the managing body and external agencies, the lack of proper reporting delays the process of planning and decision-making related to achieving public safety.
To address the identified problems, the researchers suggest developing a Web-Based Crime Mapping System with GIS Integration for the Philippine National Police Koronadal Station to address the identified issues. The system will provide features that will allow them to properly store, manage, and monitor the collected data. It will enable the officers to enter information about crime incidents, visualize the recorded incidents on a map, and generate reports in terms of barangay and hotspots. Ultimately, this proposed information system will support the station in improving its current status and helping it perform its duties more efficiently in order to achieve peace and order in the city.
Purpose and Description of the Study
The aim of the proposed Web-Based Crime Mapping System with GIS Integration is to enhance the recording, monitoring, and reporting of crimes in Philippine National Police Koronadal Station. This project is proposed to solve the problems in the current system, which are: time-consuming encoding process, disorganized, and non-systematized records and files, inability to easily retrieve information about incidents, lack of mapping tools, delay in report generation, and no system to monitor crime records. It is also expected that this project will assist PNP Koronadal Station in organizing and maintaining accurate data, minimizing manual work, and providing timely information that will aid the city in attaining peace and order.
The proposed project is a web-based crime mapping system that will enable authorized users of the Philippine National Police Koronadal Station, including the administrator, police officers, investigators, and LGU viewers, to record and manage crime incident data, track blotter workflow status, store records in a database, display incidents on the map, view records according to barangay, identify high-crime areas, upload relevant evidence, monitor audit logs, and generate reports.
The Web-Based Crime Mapping System with GIS Integration can help the Philippine National Police Koronadal Station achieve better organization of crime records, monitoring, and reporting through the system’s features. The PNP Koronadal can retrieve and analyze the data faster and more efficient than before, making the processing of related reports easier. The system can also make it more convenient for authorized users to document and report cases using the system. The Web-Based Crime Mapping System with GIS Integration can aid the PNP Koronadal in providing reliable data for planning, monitoring, and decision-making purposes for the safety of the city.
Objectives of the Study
General Objective
To design and develop a Web-Based Crime Mapping System with GIS Integration for the Philippine National Police Koronadal Station to improve crime incident recording, organize crime-related records, reduce manual recording, support crime monitoring through geospatial visualization, and enhance access to crime information for administrators, police officers, and investigators for timely monitoring, reporting, and decision-making.
Specific Objectives
Specifically, the proposed system shall consist of the following major modules:
User Login - To provide secure role-based login for administrators, police officers, investigators, and LGU viewers using email and password, including account recovery through password reset.
Dashboard - To show an overview of crime incidents, crime types, barangay hotspot summaries, case statuses, and crime trends.
User Management - To allow administrators to manage system user accounts, roles, and account status.
Crime Type Management - To allow administrators to manage crime categories used in incident recording, reports, crime mapping, and analytics.
Offense Type Management - To allow administrators to manage offense classifications related to crime types for more organized and accurate crime incident recording.
Crime Incident Management - To record, manage, and track crime incident details, including case information, offense, location, assigned officer, evidence, investigation findings, and blotter workflow status from report intake to case closure.
GIS Crime Mapping and Barangay Visualization - To plot recorded crime incidents on an interactive map and display barangay-based crime distribution, boundaries, crime density, and incident counts.
Hotspot Monitoring - To identify crime-prone areas based on recorded incidents, barangay rankings, and heatmap visualization.
Analytics Dashboard - To present crime summaries, trends, and statistical breakdowns by crime type, barangay, status, and period.
Evidence Management - To allow authorized users to upload, view, and manage supporting files related to crime incidents.
Report Generation - To generate filtered and printable crime incident reports for monitoring, documentation, and submission.
Audit Logs - To allow administrators to monitor important system activities such as incident creation, updates, status changes, evidence uploads, and archived records.
Profile Management - To allow users to update and maintain their account information.
Scope and Limitations
This study focuses on the development of a web-based Crime Mapping System with GIS Integration for the Philippine National Police Koronadal Station. The system is designed to support crime incident recording, crime data management, blotter workflow tracking, GIS-based crime mapping and barangay visualization, hotspot monitoring, analytics, evidence management, report generation, user management, audit logging, and profile management. The system aims to improve record organization, reduce manual work, support faster crime monitoring, track incident status from report intake to UCPER compilation, and provide more accessible crime information for reporting and decision-making.
Scope
User Login - This module provides secure role-based login for administrators, police officers, investigators, and LGU viewers using email and password, including account recovery through password reset.
Dashboard - This module shows an overview of crime incidents, crime types, barangay hotspot summaries, case statuses, and crime trends.
User Management - This module allows administrators to manage system user accounts, roles, and account status.
Crime Type Management - This module allows administrators to manage crime categories used in incident records, reports, maps, and analytics.
Offense Type Management - This module allows administrators to manage offense classifications related to crime types for organized crime incident recording.
Crime Incident Management - This module records, manages, and tracks crime incident details, including case information, offense, location, assigned officer, evidence, investigation findings, and blotter workflow status from report intake to case closure.

Limitations
The proposed system is subject to the following limitations:
Limited for PNP Koronadal Station Only - The system is designed specifically for the Philippine National Police Koronadal Station and may require modification before it can be used by other police stations or agencies.
Koronadal City Crime Records Only - The system covers crime incidents recorded within Koronadal City only.
Authorized User Access Only - The system can only be accessed by registered users such as administrators, police officers, investigators, and LGU viewers.
No Public or Anonymous Reporting - The system does not allow citizens or anonymous users to directly submit crime reports.
Internet and Server Dependency - The web-based system requires a working server and stable internet or local network connection to access records and map-related features.
No Separate Mobile Application - The system is available as a web-based application only and does not include a dedicated Android or iOS mobile application.
No SMS or Incident Email Notification - The system does not support SMS or email notifications for incident updates. However, email is used only for account recovery through password reset OTP verification.
No Third-Party Database Integration - The system does not connect with external government systems, national police databases, LGU systems, or other third-party data platforms. Mapping services may be used only for map visualization and location display.
No Predictive Crime Forecasting - The system supports crime recording, mapping, hotspot viewing, and analytics, but it does not include artificial intelligence, machine learning, or automated crime prediction.
Monitoring Support Only - The system is intended only to support crime recording, monitoring, mapping, and reporting, and should not replace official police investigation procedures or legal documentation processes.

Evolution and Prospects of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) Applications in Urban Crime Analysis: A Review of Literature
Mansourihanis et al. (2025) highlighted that Geographic Information Systems (GIS) have become one of the critical technologies for urban crime analysis by facilitating the collection, management, analysis and visualization of spatial data. The authors explain that the main reason for using GIS in the analysis of urban crime is that this phenomenon is a complex process, which is usually determined by some socio-economic, ecological, and demographic factors such as income inequality, ethnic and racial segregation, urbanization, immigration, land use patterns, and insufficient infrastructure. From this, it could be inferred that the analysis of urban crime has become a challenging task because of the interconnection of different factors influencing it. Therefore, researchers and local authorities could benefit from GIS technology to address this issue by creating a spatial database and visualizing crime data through mapping to identify crime hotspots and patterns. In addition, the scholars mentioned that the combination of GIS and spatial analysis has improved the understanding of crime mapping as a valuable tool for sustainable development and criminal investigation by providing a new view of the relationship between crime incidents and relevant ecological factors.
Mansourihanis et al. (2025) systematically reviewed the literature of 293 published research reports on the topic to detect patterns in the development of GIS use in urban crime detection and prevention over the last 23 years. The authors concluded that GIS was used to analyze the locations of particular crimes and identify hotspots (Mansourihanis et al., 2025). “Technological advances have made it possible to develop more sophisticated methods, including predictive policing, spatial modeling and artificial intelligence, to improve crime prevention and detection,” researchers note. Mansourihanis et al. (2025) suggest that these technologies and methods enable police to discern the patterns and trends underlying crime, and the distribution of police resources to high crime areas. The authors highlight the GIS’s role in forecasting policing, as it enables police analysts to combine data from multiple sources, including demographics, geographic information systems, road networks, social media, and historical crime statistics, to identify areas of interest for criminal activity (Mansourihanis et al., 2025). Now, this technology enables the police to prevent crime from happening by identifying areas of potential criminality before the crime is committed (Mansourihanis et al., 2025).
The review further discussed the theoretical relevance of crime prevention through environmental design (CPTED). Mansourihanis et al. (2025) explain that CPTED aims to understand how particular built environment features tend to affect criminal activity. At the same time, the researchers note that geographical information system (GIS) is applied to assess the role of the environment, including “lighting, access, street networks, center, structure, and land use.” The researchers argue that GIS can be used together with CPTED to achieve the goals of the UN sustainable development goals (UNSDG) 11 by making cities safer and improving the quality of life.
Mansourihanis et al. (2025), in their review of the potential application of artificial intelligence and geographic information systems to detect crime patterns, acknowledged the ethical concerns that come up with this technological advancement. The review highlights the benefits of employing Artificial Intelligence in the criminal justice system to make it more predictive and effective. However, the authors have mentioned some challenges in using AI and GIS for crime pattern analysis and prediction. Specifically, the review discusses the implications of such issues as ethical biases, surveillance, and privacy and proposes that these factors should be considered in making policies to govern the use of GIS in crime mapping and address other social problems affecting the community. Mansourihanis et al. (2025) conclude that GIS will continue to play an essential role in developing web-enabled crime mapping software since it offers a powerful system that empowers people using spatial information to make predictions and decisions about their communities, making it the center of effective and reliable web-based crime mapping software. As innovations continue to develop, GIS can be considered a key technology for building web-based crime mapping technologies and systems capable of meeting all the growing demands in criminal justice while enabling people to access knowledge about their localities.
GIS&T in Criminal Justice and Law Enforcement
Morgan et al. (2022) argue that Geographic information science and technology (GIS&T) has become an important component in today’s criminal justice and law enforcement due to its novel application in the collection, storage, analysis and presentation of geographical data related to criminal cases. According to Morgan et al. (2022), crimes happen in specific places and under specific circumstances. Therefore, it is fundamental to understand the locations and circumstances of criminal incidents to effectively respond to crime problems using available resources. In the chapter, Morgan et al. (2022) said that GIS&T has an important role in analyzing and visualizing of the geographic information of the criminal cases to help in effective decision-making and implement efficient strategies at different levels.
Morgan et al. (2022) provided a detailed history of the application of GIS&T in criminal justice and how far it has evolved and its importance in the modern processing of crime. As Morgan et al. (2022) stated, the first use of GIS&T in criminal justice was when it was used to manually mark crime location spots on a map for better representation and visualization. With the development of geographic technology, GIS has added to the analysis of statistical data, processing of crime maps, hot spot detection, and resolution of crime problems such as offender patterns and the association between environmental features and criminal activity (Morgan et al., 2022). Morgan et al. (2022) argues that, thanks to modern technologies, mapping technology, digital data, spatial databases, web-enabled geographic information systems, imaging systems, and satellite systems are capable of being utilized to analyze and respond to criminal incidents. This is important in helping law enforcers detect and respond to new criminal activities quickly, contributing to greater public safety in general. Morgan et al. (2022) state that GIS has become an essential tool in today’s law enforcement and criminal intelligence analysis because it can provide analytical and decision-making capabilities in crime control and security matters.
In his discussion, Morgan et al. (2022) identified several theories that provide a theoretical basis for applying GIS&T in criminal justice, namely, Environmental Criminology, Crime Pattern Theory, Routine Activity Theory, and Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design. These theories focus on the importance of the location and the environment in which crimes occur (Morgan et al., 2022). They also help to explain how the physical and social environments influence criminal activity, with little focus on the role of offenders. According to Morgan et al (2022), the theories have helped crime analysts understand the role of land use, roads, buildings, shops, homes, access to and from crime scenes and other elements that contribute to criminal activities. Morgan et al. (2022) argue that the convergence of geographical analysis and criminological theory can provide law enforcement and policy makers with an integrated understanding of the causes and correlates of criminal activity. Therefore, the application of these theories is important in helping agencies and policymakers make informed evidence-based policies and operational decisions in the area of crime prevention and control.
Morgan et al. (2022) found that the use of web-based GIS for criminal intelligence analysis and response to public safety issues has increased exponentially in the modern era. Web-based GIS provides access to the information they need thru intuitive users’ interfaces and tools available online to authorized users, partners and the public to collaborate in solving crime problems (Morgan et al., 2022). This is critical in enhancing transparency by making the information accessible and available whenever it is needed while facilitating effective coordination of criminal intelligence analysis activities among law enforcers, authorities, academics, and the public. Morgan et al. (2022) argued that web-based GIS is increasingly employed to disseminate crime-related information and decision making at different levels of implementation, offering an opportunity to share location-based information and respond quickly to emerging issues. The combined effect of the adoption of web-based GIS technologies is to enable sharing and visualization of geographical information related to criminal activity and thus achieve seamless coordination to support public safety operations.
Morgan et al. (2022) concluded their discussion with an emphasis on the fact that GIS&T has transformed the criminal justice system by providing modern technology for collecting, analyzing, and visualizing geographical data of crime. As Morgan et al. (2022) state, modern GIS&T has moved from mapping and displaying the data to offering analytical tools that allow prediction, response, and prevention of crime. Morgan et al. (2022) argue that GIS has become an integral component of criminal intelligence analysis and is useful in hotspot analysis, patrol operations, emergency response and in the development of effective strategies and policies for public safety. The authors concluded that the future of crime mapping and analysis will be greatly affected by the use of web-enabled GIS technologies, with new innovations in GIS technology enhancing decision-making and supporting efficient strategies to increase public safety and promote coexistence and sustainable development.
Predicting and Analyzing Crime–Environmental Design Relationship via GIS-Based Machine Learning Approach
Bediroglu and Colak (2023) identified that crime prediction has become possible due to the use of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and machine learning that allow researchers to investigate the associations between crime incidents and the urban environment. The authors stressed the significance of crime as a social problem that substantially impacts the lives of people and communities. In other words, there are complex interconnections between various individual, social, physical, and environmental factors and citizens’ wellbeing, public safety, and satisfaction with the quality of life. Researchers have difficulties analyzing such data with the help of traditional statistical methods. Instead, collecting and structuring data related to possible incidents and utilizing it in combination with GIS and machine learning allows predicting the spread of criminal activities and better understand their patterns. In particular, the authors highlighted that GIS is an appropriate spatial decision-making support system that helps visualize and evaluate incidents of crime with the use of relevant environmental data.
Bediroglu and Colak (2023), in particular, examined the interrelations between burglaries and the urban environment in the city of Trabzon, Turkey. Using the tools of GIS and machine learning, the authors predicted possible crimes and investigated how burglaries are affected by different environmental variables. It was identified that the Random Forest Regression (RFR) model demonstrated better results than the Support Vector Regression (SVR) algorithm. Indeed, RFR yielded an R2 value of 78%, while the R2 value of SVR was 71%. In addition, the analysis revealed that building height, floor area ratio, building density, and street intersection density have the most profound impact on burglaries, whereas the percentage of park area in comparison to residential area has a negligible effect on the incidents. The authors concluded that using the principles of GIS and regression models helps appropriately predict burglaries while providing insight into the influence of the urban environment on criminal activity.
Furthermore, Bediroglu and Colak (2023) noted that researchers could also utilize Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (CPTED) to analyze the influence of the built environment on the patterns of criminal activity. They mentioned that CPTED is a theoretical basis that assists in developing and implementing policies that improve the impact of the built environment on citizen safety and decrease threats and risks of incidents. In other words, CPTED principles help design specific areas considering the entry and exit of people, visibility within the site, surveillance rates, control of natural and artificial landscapes, and other elements that could limit the possibility of crimes. Moreover, the authors identified Crime Pattern Theory, Routine Activity Theory, Defensible Space Theory, and Space Syntax as additional concepts that highlight the necessity to consider the urban environment for better protection of the public and safer communities. The integration of GIS and CPTED allows researchers to address the role of spatial analysis and better understand the impact of different built-environment features, such as streets, roads, buildings, land use, density, lighting, and spaces, on the patterns and incidents of crime.
Bediroglu and Colak (2023) described their own methodology that was used to predict burglaries and identify the influence of the urban environment on them. The authors used burglary-related data collected by the Trabzon Police Department for the past five years and integrated it with physical evidence obtained from cadastral, municipal departments, and GIS laboratories. In particular, the data concerning crime incidents was geocoded and stored in a GIS database. Then, the authors divided the study area into 200 x 200 m grids to improve results and utilized the collected geographical data about the urban environment. It was subjected to spatial analysis with the help of GIS software. Finally, the acquired findings were examined using machine learning in Python Scikit-learn. The authors concluded that the combination of GIS and machine learning could be used as a reliable method to predict burglary crimes and examine the impact of different built-environment factors. In addition, the article stressed the necessity of further development of innovative, interactive, web-based crime analysis tools based on GIS, machine learning algorithms, and crime prevention theories to ensure safe and sustainable communities and better address the patterns of criminal activity.
Mapping Study Using the Unsupervised Learning Clustering Approach
Azis et al. (2021) assert that crime is a serious social issue that affects public safety and the country’s development. They point out that the number of criminal cases is growing rapidly and significantly in Indonesia, which requires the use of innovative analytical approaches to identify patterns and better understand crime trends. The researchers argue that traditional statistical analysis tools are not always effective in providing critical insights into crime-related data, especially when dealing with complex sets. Therefore, they suggest using data mining techniques to extract valuable information from gigantic data sets. In particular, Azis et al. (2021) propose to apply clustering algorithms to group similar types of crime and help the government and police visualize patterns associated with specific locations. Such an approach would facilitate creating appropriate policies and strategies to prevent and control further criminal activity on national levels.
In the given research, the authors used an unsupervised learning method called K-Medoids clustering analysis to examine the crime data collected from the Operations Control Bureau of the National Police Headquarters of the Republic of Indonesia and the Central Statistics Agency (BPS). Azis et al. (2021) retrieved the crime data from all provincial police regional headquarters (34 total) for the years 2017 – 2019 and used RapidMiner software to perform the clustering analysis. They determined that the Davies-Bouldin Index (DBI) was appropriate to evaluate the results of the clustering by dividing the provinces into two clusters, high-crime regions, and low-crime regions. The authors’ results indicated that six provinces fell into the category of high-crime regions while twenty-eight provinces fell into the category of low-crime regions, which demonstrated that data mining could be used to group the relevant information together in order to visualize patterns within the data set.
The researchers also discuss the relevance of data mining and clustering to crime mapping and public safety planning. Azis et al. (2021) argue that clustering helps address crimes in provinces with comparable patterns, thus saving time and effort in determining the best crime control strategies. They provide their findings on the application of clustering in scatter plots and mapping to improve the understanding of the distribution of crimes by the authorities. The researchers conclude that the combination of crime data and spatial clustering can benefit the government in monitoring and controlling criminal activities.
Azis et al. (2021) found that using K-Medoids clustering to classify crime data resulted in successfully dividing it into high- and low-crime categories with a Davies-Bouldin Index of 0.876 (a satisfactory level of clustering). The authors noted that the discovered clusters could help the government focus on high-crime areas and emphasized the importance of using data mining and machine learning methods for analyzing crime to facilitate decision-making, strengthen crime prevention, and create intelligent crime mapping systems that would assist the authorities in planning and policing activities.
A Systematic Review of Multi-Scale Spatio-Temporal Crime Prediction Methods
Du and Ding (2023) explained the nature and characteristics of crime by postulating that crime presents as a complex, ongoing, and dynamic social event that cannot be understood independently of time-space and environmental factors. As such, criminal acts usually demonstrate identifiable spatial and temporal patterns and emerge mainly from particular places referred to as hotspots (Du & Ding, 2023). Notably, contemporary crime analysis practices take cognizance of this understanding of crime to ensure effective crime prevention, police resource management, and community safety. These researchers argue that the task of predicting crime entails examining historical crime data alongside environmental, socioeconomic, and social network characteristics in order to ascertain patterns likely to emerge in particular places and times.
The researchers undertook this study with the intent to engage and review published literature on the prediction of crime over a specified period of time. Hence, Du and Ding (2023) conducted a PRISMA-based systematic review of 79 articles published between 2013 and 2022, which met the inclusion criteria for this review (Du & Ding, 2023). Following peer-review and inclusion in databases of the top journals, each of the papers that were included in this review presented different aspects of crime prediction based on content, crime type, prediction approaches, and evaluation criteria. For instance, the review grouped crime prediction models and methods as short/medium/long temporal prediction, micro/meso/macro spatial prediction, and spatio-temporal prediction, thus covering the general theory of crime prediction. The researchers also provided an overview of the most common assessment criteria used in this field, namely; Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Predictive Accuracy Index (PAI), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Accuracy, and Area Under the Curve (AUC)).
Du and Ding (2023) also identified machine learning and crime mapping as the mainstay theoretical frameworks used in contemporary crime prediction practices. According to Du and Ding (2023), researchers usually apply a range of supervised, unsupervised, and semi-supervised machine learning techniques to predict crime patterns. These machine learning algorithms include but are not limited to Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Ensemble, neural networks, and deep learning (Du & Ding, 2023). On the crime mapping front, Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) and Risk Terrain Modeling (RTM) are the most common methods used in hotspot identification, assessment of potential environmental risk factors, and representation of this information in graphical form. These researchers argue that the application of Geographic Information Systems –based data mining approaches in tandem with machine learning algorithms can help improve the identification of hotspots, which in turn enhances policing efficacy.
The researchers made several recommendations for improving crime prediction practices based on current gaps in the literature. First, Du and Ding (2023) noted the challenges associated with the current application of spatio-temporal data science in policing, including sparse data, limited interpretability, and the use of different evaluation criteria. As such, this reviewer recommends adopting cutting-edge deep artificial intelligence to address the sparse data challenges, including transformers. Additionally, this researcher recommends adopting XAI (Explainable Artificial Intelligence) approaches like SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) to improve model interpretability (Du & Ding, 2023). Ultimately, Du and Ding (2023) conclude that using Geographic Information Systems and artificial intelligence –based crime prediction practices is the way forward in future criminal justice practice and research.

Buil-Gil et al. (2022) highlighted the importance of micro-level analysis based on very local scales like street segments, addresses, and places in crime research. Authors explained that the distribution of crime is not random since criminal activity tends to concentrate in particular places. It was explained that a large percentage of crimes occur in only a fraction of space, and police forces acknowledge this fact and are starting to shift towards place-based policing. The authors added that crime analysis using geographical information systems (GIS) and mapping is considered an excellent approach to identifying hotspots and better allocating police resources.
Buil-Gil et al. (2022) argued that although micro-local levels of geographical analysis offer greater resolution and granularity, such information can be associated with significant measurement issues like varying levels of police-recorded crime. Authors explained that victim characteristics like age, gender, education, ethnicity, employment status, and others affect people’s willingness to report criminal activity to the authorities. Similarly, the level of police presence, surveillance, and monitoring activities, as well as general attitudes towards police and feelings of safety, are crucial determinants of reported crime in a given area. Therefore, a combination of differing police forces’ strategies, surveillance, recording practices, and residents’ perceptions of safety creates significant variations in police-reported crime statistics and an altered understanding of the crime reality in a given location.
Buil-Gil et al. (2022) conducted a simulation study to demonstrate how police data can skewer the analysis of geographical variation and distribution of crime. The authors applied GIS software and statistical analysis to evaluate differences in police data at various levels of geographical aggregation – Output Areas, Lower Super Output Areas, Middle Super Output Areas, and Wards.
Buil-Gil et al. (2022) determined that police data results in substantially increasing measurement error, especially at fine levels of geographical aggregation. Authors found that police-recorded crime data varied significantly at different levels of address-level resolution and was substantially lower than expected for larger statistical areas. The authors explained that such inconsistencies in police records hinder effective hotspot detection and appropriate resource allocation to modulate crime levels and patterns. Buil-Gil et al. Karmakar (2024) discussed the concept of a crime hotspot by asserting that criminal activities are non-random events and tend to concentrate in specific locations due to particular environmental attributes. The author revealed that the hotspot analysis technique has helped police forces understand the association between the environment and criminal activities. Karmakar (2024) asserted that the main reasons why crimes occur in specific locations include; city design, social and economic factors, accessibility to targets, and the ease of escape after committing the offence. According to the author, hotspot analysis has helped law enforcers identify the location patterns of crimes to enable them design efficient policing strategies.
Karmakar (2024) emphasized the significance of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) software in hotspot analysis to help police forces determine the most critical areas to monitor and control criminal activities. Karmakar (2024) further argued that GIS technology has enhanced hotspot analysis by enabling the identification of the relationship between the environment and criminal activities. The author added that hotspot analysis helps police forces make informed policing decisions based on generated statistics, insights, and trends.
In hotspot analysis, Karmakar (2024) identified several algorithms used in ArcGIS software, including Kernel Density Estimation (KDE), Getis-Ord Gi Hot Spot Analysis, Cluster and Outlier Analysis ( Anselin Local Moran’s I), and Optimized Hot Spot Analysis (OHA). The author explained that KDE works by determining the densities of crime incidents and generating a density surface to facilitate hotspot analysis and identification of high-crime areas. According to Karmakar (2024), Getis-Ord Gi Hot Spot Analysis helps detect hotspots, coldspots, and outliers based on the similarity of nearby features by adopting a sliding window approach. The author added that Cluster and Outlier Analysis (Anselin Local Moran’s I) is useful in hotspot analysis as it helps identify high and low clusters of a given phenomenon with their corresponding outliers.
On the other hand, Karmakar (2024) revealed that Optimized Hot Spot Analysis (OHA) helps determine the most suitable search window size for each feature based on the population density or cluster size identified in the study area. The author further asserted that Emerging Hot Spot Analysis is an efficient approach to hotspot analysis as it helps detect and analyze emerging, disappearing, and consistent hotspots and coldspots of criminal activities. According to Karmakar (2024), this approach involves comparing observed results with expected results across time to identify consistent patterns and changes in the hotspots of crime.
Karmakar (2024) reviewed prior studies on the significance of hotspot analysis and GIS in policing. The author evaluated previous research to reveal the effectiveness of using GIS-based spatial analysis approaches in crime analysis and prevention. Karmakar (2024) identified several studies that proved beyond reasonable doubt that most crimes always occur in specific locations, streets, and premises. The author added that prior research had demonstrated that police forces could achieve significant results in preventing crimes by focusing their efforts on the hotspots identified through hotspot analysis. Karmakar (2024) asserted that many studies had shown the usefulness of hotspot analysis in policing while recommending the need for improved crime statistics and enhanced adoption of hotspot analysis to achieve sustainable crime reduction strategies.
After reviewing the approaches and significance of hotspot analysis in policing, Karmakar (2024) revealed the limitations associated with hotspot analysis as a policing mechanism. The author explained that hotspot analysis faces several challenges in identifying and analyzing crime hotspots, thereby hindering its effectiveness in crime prevention. Karmakar (2024) asserted that several shortcomings in hotspot analysis could be overcome by adopting more reliable and efficient datasets on crimes. The author argued that the accuracy of hotspot analysis highly depends on the reliability and quality of data used in the study. According to Karmakar (2024), some critical challenges in hotspot analysis include; incomplete data, the impact of aggregated data, and the reliability of data sources on the overall effectiveness of hotspot analysis in policing. As a result, the author recommended the need for ongoing and continuous research on crime hotspot analysis and suggested that collaboration between law enforcers, researchers, and the community would enhance effective hotspot analysis and policing.

Kebede et al. (2025) adopted a concurrent mixed method research design to reveal the patterns of crime by analysing data both quantitatively and qualitatively. The authors used Hot spot analysis, Moran’s I Spatial autocorrelation, Semivariogram Modeling, Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW), and Standard Deviation Ellipse (SDE) spatial statistical models. The study adopted descriptive and exploratory sequential procedures to explore the relationships between various factors and crime events and assess the patterns of spatial randomness and non-randomness of the incidents. Kebede et al. (2025) achieved their objective of examining the spatial distribution of 2018-2021 crime incidents in Dessie City by adopting concurrent mixed methods and employing ArcGIS Pro software.
The authors examined the spatial clustering patterns of the identified crime incidents and established that the spatial distribution of incidents was not random. Kebede et al. (2025) revealed that Hote, Arada, and Segno were high-crime hotspots, whereas Menafesha and Bounbouwuha were the cold spots in Dessie City. Moran’s I spatial autocorrelation results showed a positive Moran’s Index of 0.380984, an associated z-score of 3.297616, and a p-value of less than 0.01, providing statistically significant evidence that crime incidents occurred non-randomly at a local level. Moreover, the authors established that the majority of the identified crime incidents were related to theft and that the incidents occurred mostly during the day. Kebede et al. (2025) adopted Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) interpolation to predict the likely hotspots and coldspots of crime and hypothesized that the central business district of Dessie City would be the next major crime hotspot because of the presence of many businesses, bus stands, markets, and dense populations.
Kebede et al. (2025) revealed that most crime incidents exhibited a north-to-south directional distribution, whereas murders showed a northeast-to-southwest directional distribution using Standard Deviation Ellipse analysis. The authors concluded that the use of GIS-based crime mapping approaches in combination with any of the above-mentioned spatial models can help improve the identification of crime hotspots and patterns, predict future incidents, and enhance spatial crime analysis in general. Kebede et al. (2025) recommend that policing agencies use GIS-based approaches to improve hotspot detection, patrol operations, and resource allocation while promoting effective crime prevention and control strategies in study areas.

A Spatial Analysis of Crime Trends and Solution Efficiency across Philippine Police Regional Offices
Sangco et al. (2026) performed a national-scale spatial analysis to identify patterns of crime and solution efficiency and their relationship with socio-economic characteristics in the country. The authors’ main point is that addressing the patterns of crime and their solution in the context of both national and international concerns is necessary for the development of effective policing strategies at national administrative levels, especially for the territory as diverse and complex as the Philippines. Previous research attempted to address the issue of identifying hotspots of crime, yet the authors note the absence of nationwide analyses, and they attempt to fill this gap by utilizing GIS and spatial statistical analysis to study the interplay between crimes, their resolution, and socio-economic factors.
Sangco et al. (2026) conducted the study by retrieving crime incident and crime solved records from the Philippine National Police between January 2022 and September 2023. The researchers accessed socioeconomic and demographic data from the Philippines Statistics Authority and the World Bank and regional shapefiles from the Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX). Sangco et al. integrated the data using GIS software. The researchers used QGIS 3.4, GeoDa, and SaTScan to carry out hotspot analysis, Global Moran’s I Spatial Autocorrelation, Spatial Lag Modeling (SPLM), and Spatial Scan Statistics to reveal the hotspots of crimes, assess the association, examine the influence of socio-economic determinants, evaluate the efficacy of solutions, and identify clusters of significantly low values of police regional offices across the country.
According to the study conducted by Sangco et.al in 2026, it was found that there were various categories of crimes including theft, robbery, rape, murder, physical injury, homicide, and carnapping which were reported throughout the duration of the study. However, it was noted that these crimes were not uniformly distributed, instead, hotspots were identified for most of the crimes while some regions had lower instances of criminal activity. The National Capital Region, CALABARZON, Central Luzon, and Central Visayas were found to have the highest occurrences of major crimes such as robbery, theft, rape, and carnapping. In contrast, CAR, MIMAROPA, and BARMM were determined to be less criminal due to their lack of substantial urban development. It was concluded by the researchers that highly urbanized and developed areas offer more opportunities for individuals to commit crimes due to the increased level of interactions taking place in these regions.
Sangco et al. (2026) utilized Global Moran’s I Spatial Autoregression in order to determine whether instances of crime were distributed randomly or not. According to the results, there was an occurrence of a positive spatial autocorrelation, which was determined by Moran’s I value of 0.384, p-value of 0.000. This implies that instances of crime were not randomly distributed throughout the regions of the Philippines. Further analysis using the Spatial Lag Model revealed that unemployment and population are positive contributors to the incidence of crime, while the number of police stations is a negative contributor to the incidence of crime, which implies that higher numbers of police stations lead to significantly lower levels of crime.
Sangco et al. (2026) also performed Spatial Scan Statistics in order to detect the existence of statistically significant low-risk (cold spot) clusters. As a result, three large low clusters were identified, which were significantly lower than the expected number of incidents. The researchers concluded that the detected areas had a considerably low risk level compared to the neighboring sites. Overall, the authors highlight the importance of combining GIS, hotspot mapping, spatial autocorrelation, spatial regression, and spatial clustering analysis to study patterns of crime and police performance adequately. The researchers recommended focusing on improving police activity in high-crime urban settings, increasing the level of community policing and interagency cooperation, and promoting educational and employment opportunities as the key measures to improve the current state of affairs in the Philippines.

Synthesis 
The reviewed literature and related studies reveal that advances in developing GIS for crime analysis are apparent in its spatial accuracy, predictability, artificial intelligence utilization, web accessibility, and theoretical soundness. Foreign studies focus on explainable predictions, enhanced hotspot analysis, and interactive web mapping, as well as implementing machine learning, environmental design theories, and other crime prevention tactics. Local studies centered on the need for applying GIS for effective law enforcement, allocating resources, and engaging communities in public safety across the country. GIS-based crime analysis is still constrained by data discrepancies, biases, inconsistencies, and limited application of spatial analysis in local communities.
The reviewed literature provides substantial theoretical and methodological basis for developing GIS-based crime analysis in improving hotspot identification, spatial accuracy, and community engagement in local government units (LGUs). Mansourihanis et al. (2025), Morgan et al. (2022), and Dukiya (2021) agree that criminal activity has spatial patterns that can be predicted using environmental, social, and demographic variables and theories such as Environmental Criminology, Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design, Routine Activity, and Social Disorganization. Bediroglu and Colak (2023) improved the accuracy of spatial analysis using machine learning algorithms in Random Forest Regression and Support Vector Regression, achieving a 78% prediction accuracy in determining the environmental factors for burglaries. Meanwhile, Azis et al. (2021) utilized K-Medoids clustering in unsupervised machine learning to identify spatial patterns associated with high and low-crime locations, and Du and Ding (2023) provided an extensive list of spatio-temporal prediction models such as Kernel Density Estimation and Risk Terrain Modeling. Karmakar (2024) discussed spatial statistics, including Getis-Ord Gi, Anselin Local Moran’s I, and Optimized Hot Spot Analysis, and Kebede et al. (2025) analyzed spatio-temporal patterns of crimes using similar software in Dessie City, Ethiopia. They found that the study area had significant spatial clustering of incidents, and interpolation methods accurately predicted potential crime areas.
Various studies tried to enhance hotspot determination, visualization, and accessibility in developing crime-mapping systems and solutions. Kim et al. (2025) employed Explainable Artificial Intelligence in creating an accessible web-based system that utilizes extreme gradient boosting and SHAP values to explain the model and provide insights into the identified environmental and urban factors affecting criminal activity. Cafaro et al. (2026) proposed a hotspot detection framework that uses a Weighted Fuzzy C-Means clustering algorithm that considers the density of data points and improves the accuracy of the conventional clustering approach while maintaining a short computation time. On the other hand, Spencer (2026) argued that using dynamic and interactive web-based mapping visualization tools is better than static images because it allows multi-scale analysis to understand the changing patterns of crime over time.
However, several studies identified limitations and challenges in implementing GIS-based crime analysis systems and solutions. Yusuf et al. (2025) conducted a systematic review of published literature on the application of GIS in enhancing crime analysis in sub-Saharan Africa and found significant research gaps in the region, such as the lack of standardized geocoding processes and proper infrastructure and expertise in utilizing GIS technology. Similarly, Buil-Gil et al. (2022) found that recording and reporting biases affect the accuracy of micro-area analysis and hotspot identification in several European countries. The findings of Yusuf et al. (2025) and Buil-Gil et al. (2022) can be used as a critical consideration in this study since several LGUs in the Philippines also experience similar issues in adopting GIS-based crime analysis.
Studies conducted in the Philippines to improve community safety and public security through GIS-based solutions highlight the need to develop an accessible system that can maximize the use of existing tools while addressing the limitations of current practices. Pilotin (2025) used QGIS in analyzing and visualizing the hotspots and crime trends in Apayao Province to determine the best locations for installing closed-circuit television (CCTV) cameras. The study achieved positive feedback from the community as it increased the rate of crime clearance and solution after the installation of CCTV devices based on the recommendations of the analysis. Sangco et al. (2026) performed a spatial analysis of intentional crime incidence in the Philippines using QGIS, GeoDa, and SaTScan software. They found that the country exhibits positive spatial autocorrelation across all regions, with theft being the most prevalent type of offense, affecting densely populated areas with a high unemployment rate. Lastly, Laurena et al. (2022) designed a web-based citizen-initiated incident reporting and tracking system using Google Maps API that provides the public with a platform to report crime and track the progress of their reports. The system received positive feedback from the citizens and LGUs due to its convenience and accessibility.
Based on the reviewed literature, several similarities can be drawn among the studies in terms of objectives, research questions, methodology, and scope. Most of the foreign studies mentioned above employed similar hotspot detection and identification technologies, including spatial statistics, machine learning clustering algorithms, and web-based visualization techniques. Some of these studies utilized related crime theories, particularly the application of Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design in addressing community safety issues. Others built upon the findings of previous studies, such as those conducted by Yusuf et al. (2025) and Buil-Gil et al. (2022), by including data bias as one of the variables affecting crime patterns and integrating it into their analysis. On the other hand, Spencer (2026) focused on creating an interactive, multi-scale mapping application that could better display the analysis results to the users than the static visuals produced by most GIS software.
The reviewed foreign literature and related studies present several limitations and recommendations for future research on GIS-based crime analysis. Most of the studies conducted abroad had a set objective to enhance hotspot identification, making the results too specific to determine the broader impact of utilizing GIS in improving areas related to community safety, law enforcement, and crime prevention. Most of these studies came from countries with better technology and resources in adopting GIS than the Philippines, making them difficult to implement in local government units. The findings of Yusuf et al. (2025) and Buil-Gil et al. (2022) can partially apply to the Philippine setting since some LGUs face similar challenges in adopting GIS-based crime analysis systems. However, the studies made specific recommendations based on the unique needs of their countries, which may not be cost-effective when applied to the Philippines. Most foreign studies focused only on one aspect of GIS-based crime analysis, such as enhancing hotspot identification and applying it to a particular region. There is a need to develop a system or framework that integrates different analyses and makes it accessible to the general public and local government units in terms of cost and complexity.
Based on the research gap identified in the reviewed literature and related studies, this study aims to propose a GIS-Based Integrated Crime Mapping and Analysis System that local government units can utilize in addressing community safety and crime prevention issues. The suggested system should be able to provide spatial information, identify hotspots efficiently and effectively, perform fundamental predictive analysis, and manage incident data while being accessible to the public in terms of its web and mobile-based interface. Furthermore, implementing this study will ensure that citizens can report incidents conveniently while addressing the issues affecting the accuracy of data and records in current practices and fully utilizing the theories and principles related to crime prevention through environmental design.

Non-Functional Requirements
Performance Requirements
REQ. 1.1 The system shall load pages, render the crime map, and display dashboard and analytics charts within an acceptable response time under normal network conditions.
REQ. 1.2 The system shall process incident creation, incident updates, workflow status transitions, report generation, and filtering actions without noticeable delay to the user.
REQ. 1.3 The system shall handle a growing volume of crime incident records, evidence files, audit logs, and user accounts without significant performance degradation.
Security Requirements
REQ. 2.1 The system shall ensure that only authorized users can access modules and functions based on their assigned role, including Admin, Police Officer, Investigator, and LGU users.
REQ. 2.2 The system shall encrypt user passwords and protect login credentials from unauthorized access.
REQ. 2.3 The system shall prevent users from viewing, editing, archiving, or managing records outside their assigned role permissions.
REQ. 2.4 The system shall validate and restrict uploaded evidence files to prevent malicious or unauthorized file types.
REQ. 2.5 The system shall automatically terminate the user session after logout and prevent access to authenticated pages without logging in again.
Usability Requirements
REQ. 3.1 The system shall provide a clean and understandable interface that allows authorized users to navigate modules and perform tasks with minimal training.
REQ. 3.2 The system shall provide clear error messages, success notifications, and confirmation prompts to guide user actions and prevent accidental data changes.
REQ. 3.3 The system shall present dashboards, tables, charts, reports, and map views in an organized and readable format.
Reliability Requirements
REQ. 4.1 The system shall remain stable and available during police station and LGU operating hours.
REQ. 4.2 The system shall accurately maintain crime incident records, workflow history, evidence information, audit logs, and user activity records.
REQ. 4.3 The system shall consistently display accurate incident, evidence, map, dashboard, analytics, and report data based on the user’s role and access level.
Availability Requirements
REQ. 5.1 The system shall be accessible to authorized users during intended operating hours except during scheduled maintenance or unexpected technical interruptions.
REQ. 5.2 The system shall notify users of scheduled downtime or maintenance when possible.
Scalability Requirements
REQ. 6.1 The system shall support an increasing number of barangays, crime types, offense types, user accounts, evidence files, and crime incident records as system usage expands.
REQ. 6.2 The system database and crime mapping module shall support an increasing volume of plotted crime incidents without significant performance loss.
Privacy Requirements
REQ. 7.1 The system shall protect sensitive crime incident data, evidence files, account information, and personal information related to complainants, victims, and personnel.
REQ. 7.2 The system shall restrict access to case details, evidence files, and user information to authorized roles only.
REQ. 7.3 The system shall record and monitor access to sensitive records through audit logs for accountability.
Compatibility Requirements
REQ. 8.1 The system shall be compatible with modern web browsers such as Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, and Mozilla Firefox on desktop and laptop devices.
REQ. 8.2 The system’s crime mapping feature shall function correctly with supported map services and standard browser-based mapping tools.
Maintainability Requirements
REQ. 9.1 The system shall use a modular structure so that modules such as Login, Dashboard, Crime Incident, Crime Map, Hotspot, Analytics, Reports, and User Management can be maintained and updated efficiently.
REQ. 9.2 The system shall maintain clear audit trails, organized records, and readable system logs to support troubleshooting, monitoring, and future system improvements.
Design of Software, Systems, Product, and/or Process
Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework for the Web-Based Crime Mapping System is explained using the Input-Process-Output (IPO) Model. This model shows the main parts of the system and how the inputs are changed into outputs through organized processes. It helps provide a clear and structured flow in the system development, focusing on data input, system functions, and the generation of results for administrators, police officers, investigators, and LGU users.
Input
User Requirements– The system provides a convenient and efficient way to record and manage crime incidents through a web-based crime mapping and monitoring system. It ensures easy access to crime data records, organized storage of crime data and evidence, and fast retrieval of crime records and reports. It also includes an interactive map for viewing crime locations and hotspots, role-based access for authorized users, and printable crime incident reports for monitoring and documentation.
Back-End Knowledge – The system is built using PHP with the Laravel framework, following the MVC architecture. It utilizes Laravel authentication and role-based access control to manage user permissions, along with file upload handling for evidence and audit logging to track system activities.
Database Knowledge – The system uses MySQL for database management, with a structured database design and defined table relationships. It handles crime incident data storage and management, including records for users, roles, evidence, barangays, crime types, offense types, and audit logs.
Front-End Knowledge – The system uses HTML, Tailwind CSS, and JavaScript, along with Laravel Blade templates, to build a responsive web design. Leaflet.js and OpenStreetMap are used to enable interactive crime mapping.
Hardware Knowledge – The system requires a monitor with at least 1366 x 768 resolution, a processor capable of running a local web server, at least 4 GB of RAM, at least 120 GB of storage, a keyboard and mouse, and an internet or local network connection.
Software Knowledge – The system runs on the Windows operating system using XAMPP, PHP, Composer, and the Laravel framework, with MySQL for database management. Node.js and npm, Visual Studio Code, and Google Chrome are used for development, alongside Leaflet.js and OpenStreetMap for the mapping features.

Application Tier
The proposed system is composed of the following components:
User Management – Manages the data of system users, including administrators, police officers, investigators, and LGU users. It handles CRUD operations for user accounts, roles, passwords, and account status.
Dashboard Management – Provides summarized information about crime incidents, crime types, barangay hotspots, case statuses, pending cases, and crime trends.
Crime Incident Management – Manages crime incident records. Authorized users can create, view, update, search, filter, archive, and track crime incidents, including case assignments, evidence, and investigation findings.
Crime Status Management – Manages the status of crime cases from report intake to case closure. Authorized users can update case statuses, while administrators can approve or reject final case status requests.
Evidence Management – Manages supporting files related to crime incidents. Authorized users can upload, view, categorize, and delete evidence files associated with a case.
Crime Mapping Management – Manages the geographical visualization of recorded crime incidents. It plots crime incidents on an interactive map and displays barangay boundaries, crime distribution, crime density, and incident counts.
Hotspot Monitoring – Manages the identification and visualization of crime-prone areas based on recorded crime incidents, barangay rankings, and map-based information.
Analytics Management – Manages crime statistics and analytical information. It provides crime summaries, trends, and statistical breakdowns based on crime type, barangay, case status, and selected periods.
Crime Type Management – Manages the crime categories used in recording, mapping, reporting, and analyzing crime incidents.
Offense Type Management – Manages offense classifications associated with crime types to ensure organized and accurate crime incident recording.
Notification Management – Manages system notifications for important case activities, such as case assignments and case approval requests.
Report Management – Generates filtered, printable, and exportable crime incident reports for monitoring, documentation, and submission.
Audit Log Management – Records and monitors important system activities, including incident updates, evidence actions, approvals, imports, and archived records.
Profile Management – Manages user account information and allows users to update their profile details and passwords.
